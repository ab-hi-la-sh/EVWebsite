Very Good Security (VGS) and Basis Theory are both developer-first vault platforms that keep card data out of your own systems by replacing it with aliases or tokens held in a PCI-scoped vault.

VGS is best for teams that want to protect existing endpoints through a proxy without changing code, while Basis Theory is best for engineering teams that want to build payments on an API with programmable parts.

VGS vs Basis Theory: Quick Verdict

VGS: A proxy-first tokenization platform with inbound and outbound routes, Collect and Show components, VGS Compute, and a payments line that includes network tokens, account updater, and card issuing. Starter plans start at $1,000 a month, and Growth is by quote.

Basis Theory: A programmable payments vault with Elements, a general-purpose proxy, and Reactors that run your code. It publishes one plan at $995 a month for 20,000 tokens, and its docs label network tokens, account updater, 3D Secure, and agentic payments as Enterprise features. It has no reviews yet on G2 or Capterra.

VGS fits you if:

  • You have many existing endpoints and want to protect them with route configuration instead of code changes.
  • You want audit help from the vendor, since its Growth package lists a PCI compliance subscription and a provided auditor.
  • You need card issuing and network tokens from one vendor, and ISO 27001 and large named customers matter in your vendor review.

Basis Theory fits you if:

  • Your engineers want to build against an API, with Elements, a proxy, and Reactors that run your own code.
  • You want a published self-serve price and ISO 27001, and you are prepared to go to a quote for network tokens, account updater, and 3D Secure.
  • You are comfortable that it has no third-party reviews yet and will ask for references.

Enigma Vault fits you if:

  • A $1,000-a-month floor is more than your card volume justifies, and you want to start at $0.
  • Cards still arrive by phone, or you also need to protect records, documents, and AI prompts.
  • You can live without network tokens, account updater, routing, and custom code.

Bottom Line: Very Good Security bends your existing traffic around a proxy, Basis Theory hands your engineers programmable parts, and Enigma Vault gives teams without a four-figure monthly budget a PCI-scoped vault they can start building on for free.

What VGS Charges

VGS publishes two packages. Starter plans start at $1,000 a month, and the page lists no included features, units, or overage. How VGS and Basis Theory Differ at the Core

VGS sits in your traffic and swaps data for aliases on the way in and out, whereas Basis Theory is called by your application, which keeps a token and decides what happens next. That one choice shapes how much code you change, who sits in your request path, and what you maintain.

Where the Proxy Model Helps: You can protect existing endpoints with little application code, and G2 reviewers repeatedly say they understood the proxy quickly.

Where It Costs You: G2 reviewers mention reloading routes before changes take effect, needing support for complex matching, and promoting routes between vaults by hand in YAML.

Where the API Model Helps: Only the calls you choose go through the vendor, and your engineers control exactly where tokens are created and used. The price is more explicit code in your own application.

If you have dozens of existing endpoints and little appetite for code changes, the proxy model saves time. If you would rather build the integration once and keep third parties out of the rest of your traffic, the API model is cleaner.

Where VGS and Basis Theory Differ in Practice

Card Capture on the Web and in Apps

VGS Collect provides hosted form fields for JavaScript, iOS, Android, and React Native, and VGS Show reveals stored values in a VGS-hosted component. Basis Theory’s Universal Form Elements cover web and mobile, with Apple Pay and Google Pay built in. For most checkouts either one works.

If a styled, embedded checkout is your main requirement, both offer more ready-made pieces than Enigma Vault’s hosted forms and API.

Network Tokens, Account Updater, and Routing

This is the heart of both products, and at both it sits behind a quote. Network tokens and account updater keep stored cards working after a reissue or expiry, which protects recurring revenue. Basis Theory’s docs label network tokens, account updater, Universal 3D Secure, and BIN and card-detail enrichments as Enterprise features, and its pricing page gives no prices for them. Its payments-routing guide is marked under construction, although its site markets smart and backup routing. VGS lists network tokens, account updater, and card attributes on its Growth package, runs a card management platform, and offers 3D Secure through a single API.

One thing to check with VGS: its status page lists network tokens, account updater, card attributes, 3D Secure, and file tokenization only in the US environment, while the EU and Asia-Pacific environments list a shorter set of components, mainly the proxy, dashboard, Collect, and Show. That is a monitoring list, not a published availability statement, so confirm regional availability with Very Good Security if you process outside the US.

Custom Code: VGS Compute and Basis Theory Reactors

Both let you run your own logic on sensitive data without it touching your servers. VGS Compute runs customer-written code, in a Python-like language, inside the VGS proxies on data in transit, for custom cryptographic operations, payload transformation, and policy enforcement. Basis Theory’s Reactors run your code against vaulted data inside its secure environment, for example to transform a value or send it to a partner.

Beyond Cards: PII, Files, and AI Prompts

VGS’s vault holds more than cards. Its documentation names social security numbers, passport numbers, and any other data you deem sensitive, and Managed File Tokenization handles CSV, JSON, XML, Parquet, ISO 8583, and EBCDIC files. Very Good Security also markets an AI Data Firewall, a proxy that tokenizes sensitive values in prompts, but its page lists no pricing, documentation, or entity counts. VGS’s trust center does not list HIPAA.

Basis Theory has a bank-account token type and a generic token type that stores any primitive or unstructured data, with PII and PHI compliance options on Enterprise. On the $995 plan, payloads are capped at 1 MB and logs are kept for 24 hours, so it is not a document store.

Implementation Time and Support

VGS says activating an account can take up to two business days, after which you create a live vault, promote your sandbox routes, and ask support to enable live data. G2 shows an average implementation time of about four months, although G2 reviewers also praise quick setup, so the real figure depends heavily on how many routes you protect. Reviewers often praise Very Good Security support, especially chat.

Basis Theory’s migration page promises a move from VGS “in hours,” which is a vendor claim, and its docs say network tokens, account updater, and agentic payments need onboarding before production. One editorial comparison estimates six to twelve engineering weeks for the same migration without citing a source, so treat anything between hours and weeks as unproven until you test your own routes. Basis Theory’s customer stories show a more modest timeline, such as Cubby going from proof of concept to live in about a week.

Who Should Choose VGS, Basis Theory, or Enigma Vault

A Payments Fintech or Marketplace With an Engineering Team

Both VGS and Basis Theory fit. Pick VGS if you are retrofitting traffic across many services or want audit help bundled with the vendor. Pick Basis Theory if your team would rather build against an API and use Reactors and routing. If your card volume is modest, price both against Enigma Vault first.

A Subscription or Ecommerce Business With Cards on File

Network tokens and account updater protect recurring revenue, and both Very Good Security and Basis Theory sell them, at their quote-only tiers: Growth at VGS and Enterprise at Basis Theory. If most of your revenue is recurring, that is worth paying for. If customers can re-enter an expired card, Enigma Vault covers storage and gateway charging at a fraction of the price.

An Agency, Travel Business, Clinic, or Support Team Taking Cards by Phone

A proxy protects data arriving over HTTP. It does nothing for a card number read aloud on a call and typed into a ticket. We found no phone channel on VGS’s site, and Basis Theory does not package one. Enigma Vault’s Card Vault includes Twilio Pay IVR from the Growth tier, so callers enter their card on the keypad and agents never hear it.

A Team That Wants to Try Before Buying

Very Good Security offers a free sandbox that is not for real data. Basis Theory has a free test tenant for synthetic data only, and no free production plan. Enigma Vault’s Lite tier is also a free sandbox for development and testing, with hard limits and no card to enter, so you can build against the API before you pay anything. Paid plans start at $49 a month.

Moving From VGS or Basis Theory to Enigma Vault

If the price or the feature set no longer fits, the move is a card migration between two PCI-compliant providers, which both vendors and your acquirer need to agree.

  • Map Your Routes or Tokens First: List every VGS route or Basis Theory token flow, and every downstream system that depends on the alias or token format.
  • Check What You Rely On: If you use network tokens, account updater, routing, VGS Compute, or Reactors, confirm your gateway covers them or that you can live without them.
  • Choose a Token Format: Enigma Vault issues random tokens, with a Luhn-passable option if a legacy system checks card formats.
  • Re-Tokenize and Run Both for a Few Weeks: Then cut over.
