The best Basis Theory alternatives for 2026: 1. Enigma Vault, 2. VGS, 3. Spreedly, 4. Skyflow, 5. Evervault, 6. TokenEx / IXOPAY, 7. PCI Proxy.

Staying on Basis Theory costs $995 a month before anything else happens. That is fine when you are tokenizing at volume. It is harder to justify when you are running 3,000 cards a month, or when you discover that the entry plan is US-only and your EU customers need somewhere else to live.

Basis Theory is a good product. It takes the raw card, holds it in its own PCI Level 1 vault and returns a token, with network tokens and Account Updater native to that vault. For a payments team at scale, it earns the price.

If you are here, you are likely weighing whether a $995 monthly floor, a US-only entry plan and enterprise-gated PII and PHI coverage still fit your volume, your regions and the data you now need to protect.

Why Teams Look For Basis Theory Alternatives

Basis Theory earns its place. The vault holds the card so your systems never store it, network tokens and Account Updater are native rather than bolted on, multi-PSP routing has no stated partner limitations, and a PCI Attestation of Compliance is included at the entry tier. The reasons buyers evaluate alternatives are about entry cost, regions and data scope, not the core vault.

  • The floor is $995 a month: That includes 20,000 tokens. If you are using 2,000, you are paying for 18,000 you do not need, and there is no smaller tier to drop to.
  • The entry plan is US environment only: This is the one that surprises people. If EU data residency enters your requirements, the published entry plan does not cover it and you are into an enterprise conversation.
  • PII and PHI sit on the enterprise tier: Basis Theory vaults more than cards, but the compliance options for personal and health data are not an entry-level purchase, and a team whose scope widened mid-year tends to find this out at renewal.
  • The entry tier is capped in ways that matter: A 1 MB payload limit and 24 hours of logs sound minor until you are vaulting documents or an auditor asks for longer retention.

If those points match your evaluation, the alternatives below sort cleanly by how they answer them.

How We Evaluated These Basis Theory Alternatives

We scored every provider on the same six criteria, weighted around the three reasons teams leave Basis Theory. Weights sum to 100%, and the Overall score in the table is the weighted result.

  • Entry cost and pricing transparency (25%): What the first real tier costs, and whether a smaller step exists.
  • Data residency and deployment (20%): Regions available at the entry tier, and self-hosting options.
  • Coverage beyond cards (20%): Structured PII, files and intake, and whether they are entry-level or enterprise-gated.
  • Payments depth (15%): Network tokens, Account Updater, 3DS, BIN lookup, card-present.
  • Processor independence (10%): Do tokens move between gateways?
  • Compliance evidence (10%): What each vendor publishes on its own trust pages.

This is an editorial comparison published by one of the vendors in it. The weighting is the argument, so disagree with it openly: Raise payments depth to 30% and VGS wins, raise compliance and residency and Skyflow does.

On review scores: G2 and Capterra ratings here were checked on each product's page on 16 October 2026, and every one is reported with its review count. An average drawn from six reviews and an average drawn from forty-seven are not the same claim.

Where a vendor has no published reviews, we say so plainly rather than filling the gap with something softer.

On PCI scope: Moving between PCI DSS Level 1 providers does not by itself change your scope. Scope follows your environment and integration, and your acquirer or a QSA confirms it.

2. VGS

Best for: Payments teams that want to keep network tokens, Account Updater and 3DS while changing less application code.

VGS runs a processor-agnostic vault behind a reverse proxy that aliases sensitive data before it reaches your systems, then re-inserts the real value in real time on the way out.

For a Basis Theory buyer, this is the lateral move that keeps feature parity. You get network tokens, Account Updater, 3DS and BIN lookup, and you change less of your application because the proxy sits in front of it.

Key features

  • Reverse-proxy aliasing that swaps sensitive values before they reach your servers, then re-inserts the real data on the outbound call, so your application logic does not change
  • Network tokens issued and managed for you, which keep stored credentials current and can improve authorisation rates
  • Account Updater, which refreshes expired or reissued cards automatically so recurring charges stop failing silently
  • 3D Secure, BIN lookup and account validation, covering checks most payment teams otherwise buy separately
  • Wallet payments and agentic commerce tooling for newer checkout paths
  • A processor-agnostic vault holding cards, PII and files in one place rather than across separate tools
  • An AI Data Firewall for teams routing sensitive records into model calls
  • A free tier to test the integration before the paid plan starts

Pricing

Published plans begin at $1,000 a month. There is a free tier for getting started, but the first paid step sits above the Basis Theory floor you are leaving.

Pros

  • Deepest payments feature set in this comparison
  • Less application rework than a direct vault integration
  • Covers PII and files alongside cards, without an enterprise gate

Cons

  • At $1,000 the floor is higher than Basis Theory's, so this solves nothing if cost is your reason
  • The proxy adds a network hop and an operational dependency in the payment path
  • Architecturally a bigger change than moving between two direct vault APIs

5. Evervault

Best for: Payments teams that want to model cost per operation instead of paying a flat token allowance.

Evervault is encryption-based rather than vault-based, with dual custody: You hold encrypted cards without the keys, Evervault holds keys without your cards.

The custody model is a real change from Basis Theory, which holds the card itself. Your systems end up storing ciphertext rather than a token.

Key features

  • Dual custody, the inverse of a vault model: You hold the ciphertext, Evervault holds the key
  • Secure iframes for collection and reveal, keeping raw cards off your frontend
  • Relay, an encrypted proxy decrypting in transit just before the destination API
  • Enclaves on AWS Nitro for computation over encrypted data
  • Network Tokens API, 3D Secure and Account Updater for the recurring lifecycle
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay support
  • Fingerprint, exposing card metadata and BIN details but never the number

Pricing

Platform is $995 a month plus usage, recommended for under 25,000 transactions a month. Encrypts, relays and functions are unlimited. Decrypts are $0.005 each, file decrypts $0.02, fingerprints $0.005, successful 3DS $0.03 with nothing charged for failures.

Pros

  • Dual custody is a genuinely different security posture
  • Enclave-backed processing for sensitive compute

Cons

A Note On Vaulted Versus Vaultless

Basis Theory publishes content arguing that vaultless tokenization relies on local key protection, has seen limited industry adoption, and lacks PCI DSS recognition for protecting primary account numbers. That is a vendor position from a vaulted provider, and it is worth reading alongside the vaultless vendors' own material rather than as settled fact.

The practical difference is where the original value lives and what your exit looks like. Our guide to vault vs vaultless tokenization covers both sides.

How To Choose A Basis Theory Alternative

Which constraint are you actually hitting? The $995 floor points to Enigma Vault or Spreedly. The US-only entry plan points to Enigma Vault or Skyflow. PII and PHI on enterprise points to Enigma Vault or Skyflow.

How many tokens do you really use? If it is well under 20,000 a month, you are paying for headroom. Price the tier that matches your actual volume.

Which payments features do you call? Account Updater, network tokens and BIN lookup are the features most likely to disappear in a move. Inventory them first.

Do you need more than cards? Documents, structured PII and customer intake point to Enigma Vault or Skyflow. Everything else here is payments-only.

Can you absorb a re-tokenization project? Every option means new tokens and updating every system holding one. If not this quarter, renegotiating may beat migrating.

What Migrating Off Basis Theory Actually Involves

Confirm the export path first: Get in writing how tokenized card data is exported, in what format and over what channel, while you are still a customer in good standing.

Expect a PCI-to-PCI transfer: Card data moves between two compliant environments and both providers must agree the process. Involve your acquirer early rather than at cut-over.

Your tokens do not travel: New vault, new tokens. Every system holding a Basis Theory token needs updating, which is usually more work than the new integration.

Native features become integrations: Account Updater and network tokens are native to the Basis Theory vault. On most alternatives they are a separate product or absent. Map that gap before you commit.

Dual-run before cutting over: Write to both vaults and reconcile. Budget for two vendors for a month or two.

Re-check your region requirement: If you are moving partly for EU residency, confirm the target vendor offers it at the tier you are actually buying, not just somewhere in their product line.
