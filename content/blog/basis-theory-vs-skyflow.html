Basis Theory and Skyflow are both developer-first vault platforms that keep sensitive values out of your own systems and give your engineers an API to store and use them.

Basis Theory is best for engineering teams building payments on a vault with a published price, while Skyflow is best for enterprises that need to govern sensitive data and AI workflows across a company.

This guide compares Basis Theory and Skyflow on architecture, features, pricing, compliance, and reviews, with Enigma Vault alongside as a third option for teams that want published prices and a simpler start.

Basis Theory vs Skyflow: Quick Verdict

Basis Theory: A payments-first token vault with Elements for web and mobile, a proxy, and Reactors that run Node.js against tokens. Go with Basis Theory if:

  • Payments are your product and your engineers want to build with Elements, a proxy, and Reactors.
  • You want a published starting price and ISO 27001, and you accept that network tokens and account updater mean an Enterprise conversation.
  • Your data is mostly cards and bank details, and your volume fits a stored-token price.

Go with Skyflow if:

  • You need one governed place for PII, PHI, and payment data, with different views for different teams or roles.
  • You are building AI features or agents and want de-identification and policy-based re-identification in the same platform.
  • You need customer-managed keys, a dedicated VPC, or your own cloud account, and you have an enterprise buying process.

Go with Enigma Vault if:

  • You want published prices and a free tier for cards, records, files, and prompts before any sales process.
  • Cards arrive by phone, or you want a no-code intake app for staff and customers.
  • You do not need Reactors, network tokens, or field-level policies across many teams.

The short version: Basis Theory hands payments engineers a programmable vault with a visible entry price, Skyflow governs sensitive data across a company, and Enigma Vault covers the common vaulting jobs at published prices for teams that need neither depth.

Where Basis Theory and Skyflow Differ in Practice

Developer Experience and Time to First Token

Basis Theory has a free, self-serve test tenant, a large public docs site, server SDKs for .NET, Java, Go, Node, and Python, a Terraform provider, a CLI, and a production checklist. The test tenant is not PCI compliant and takes synthetic data only.

Skyflow’s homepage call to action is a demo. Its free Try Account is request-gated, so a team member contacts you to set it up, and it allows test data only. Skyflow’s SDKs cover Java, Node.js, Python, and Go on the server and JavaScript, React, React Native, iOS, and Android on the client. If you want to run a card through a vault today without talking to anyone, Basis Theory’s test tenant gets you closer than Skyflow does.

Payments Depth and What Sits Behind Enterprise

Basis Theory’s payments tooling is its strength: network tokens, account updater, Universal 3D Secure, BIN and card-detail enrichments, and an agentic payments API. Its docs label all of these Enterprise features, and its pricing page gives no prices for them. Its payments-routing guide is marked under construction, although its site markets smart and backup routing. Elements, the proxy, and synchronous Reactors carry no plan label in its docs, so confirm what the $995 plan includes.

Skyflow’s documentation lists network tokenization, account updater, 3D Secure, and BIN lookup, and it connects to processors such as Stripe, Adyen, Braintree, Cybersource, and Checkout. It does not describe card issuing as a capability, and we found no Apple Pay or Google Pay support in its docs. If payments optimization is the point, ask each vendor to demonstrate the specific features you will use, on the plan you will buy.

Data Types, Files, and PII

Basis Theory has card and bank-account token types and a generic token type that stores any value, but payloads are capped at 1 MB on the $995 plan, and PII and PHI compliance options are Enterprise. Skyflow’s documentation covers structured and unstructured data, an Upload File API that stores files in a file column, and Detect, which de-identifies CSV, Word, PowerPoint, PDF (redaction only), audio, and images. Neither publishes a file size limit beyond Basis Theory’s payload cap.

Governance, Logs, and Keys

Basis Theory controls access through applications, permissions, and access rules, and it audits all activity through a logs API. On the $995 plan, logs are kept for 24 hours, and custom log access is Enterprise. SSO and SAML are Enterprise, and we found no customer-managed keys for the vault.

Skyflow combines roles, row-level rules, and context-aware authorization, returns values masked, redacted, or in plain text depending on who asks, and supports BYOK and BYOKMS on every deployment model. It offers an audit events API, but we found no published retention period.

AI and Agents

The two companies mean different things by agents. Basis Theory’s agent features are about agent payments: an Agentic Payments API (Enterprise) built on rails such as Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay, and PCI Agent Runtimes, which run an agent inside a Reactor. It does not mask LLM prompts.

Skyflow’s are about agent data. Detect de-identifies and re-identifies text, files, audio, and images, covers 60 entity types and more than 40 languages, and adds guardrails such as toxicity checks. It announced MCP data protection in August 2025 and launched Skyflow for Glean on 25 August 2026, while its pages on agent memory and agentic commerce have no documentation behind them that we could find.

Enigma Vault

Enigma Vault’s NoPII tokenizes PII in your application’s LLM API calls across nine or more model providers, with a free tier of one million protected tokens a month. It does not cover agent traffic.

Deployment and Data Residency

Basis Theory is hosted multi-tenant SaaS with separate test and production tenants, and we found no on-premises, self-hosted, or VPC option. Its $995 plan is US only, and EU and India regions are Enterprise.

Skyflow runs as multi-tenant SaaS, as a dedicated VPC, or as BYOC in your own cloud account on AWS or GCP. It describes region-specific vaults but publishes no region list, and its own pages cite different counts, from 100-plus regions to 150-plus countries. Enigma Vault is managed in US and EU regions, with EU residency an add-on from the Growth tier.

What Basis Theory’s Comparison Page Says About Skyflow

Basis Theory publishes its own page comparing itself with Skyflow. It makes several claims worth checking.

  • Skyflow holds PCI, SOC 2, and HIPAA. This is incomplete. Skyflow also lists ISO 27001:2022 and GDPR, and it describes its HIPAA status as “assessed and eligible.”
  • Skyflow support is a community. This looks out of date. Skyflow’s site presents Partially Redacted as a podcast, and its terms describe standard support with premium levels set in the order form.
  • Basis Theory pricing is a free trial, a per-token Starter, and a custom Professional tier. This is out of date for Basis Theory itself. Its pricing page now shows one $995 plan with a $0.05 overage and a custom Enterprise tier.
  • Skyflow has migration guides onto Skyflow but not off it. We could not verify this either way.

Skyflow has no page naming Basis Theory. Treat both as marketing, and let only the claims you can test in a proof of concept count.

Moving From Basis Theory or Skyflow to Enigma Vault

If the price or the contract terms no longer fit, the move is a data migration between two vendors, so plan it before you cancel anything.

  • Map What You Store: List every token type, field, and file, and which service or team reads each one.
  • Check What You Rely On: If you use Reactors, Functions, network tokens, account updater, or field-level policies, confirm that you can replace them or live without them.
  • Agree the Export: Basis Theory exports cards on file to another PCI-compliant provider through a support request, and Skyflow’s export goes through support as well.
  • Re-Tokenize and Run Both for a Few Weeks: Then cut over.

Our guide to migrating tokenization providers has a checklist for running two vaults side by side. If you want help mapping what you store, book a migration call.

Basis Theory and Skyflow are built for different jobs.
