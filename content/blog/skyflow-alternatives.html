The best Skyflow alternatives for 2026: 1. Enigma Vault, 2. VGS, 3. Evervault, 4. Basis Theory, 5. Spreedly, 6. TokenEx / IXOPAY, 7. Protegrity.

Skyflow is a capable data privacy vault. It isolates PII, PHI and payment data behind field-level policies, it publishes one of the longest certification lists in this market, and through 2026 it has pushed hard into AI, repositioning around what it now calls runtime AI data control.

If you are here, you are likely weighing whether a demo-gated, enterprise-first platform is the right size for what you need to protect, and whether you can size the cost without a procurement cycle.

Disclosure: Enigma Vault publishes this comparison and appears in it, first. Every competitor fact comes from that vendor's own public pages checked on 16 October 2026. Where a vendor publishes nothing, we say so rather than guessing.

Why Teams Look For Skyflow Alternatives

Skyflow earns its place. Field-level policies, broad certification coverage and deep platform integrations across Snowflake, Databricks, ServiceNow and Glean are real strengths. The reasons buyers evaluate alternatives are about price visibility, buying motion and fit, not the strength of the vault.

  • Pricing is not published: Skyflow's pricing page routes to a demo request. For a team trying to size a build, that turns a two-minute decision into a two-week procurement conversation, and it makes comparison against published-price vendors difficult.
  • The motion is enterprise-first: The integration roadmap centres on large data platforms, which is the right investment for a big estate. It is a heavier fit for a team that wants to tokenize four fields and ship this quarter.
  • The product has moved toward AI: The 2026 repositioning around runtime AI data control, AI data sanitization and agentic commerce is a coherent strategy, but if your problem is still a card number sitting in a CRM, you may be buying several steps past your requirement.

If those points match your evaluation, the alternatives below sort cleanly by how they answer them.

How We Evaluated These Skyflow Alternatives

We scored every provider on the same six criteria, weighted for a team that wants to protect sensitive data without a procurement cycle. Weights sum to 100%, and the Overall score in the table is the weighted result.

  • Pricing transparency (25%): Can you size it without a sales call, and is there a real free tier?
  • Data coverage (20%): Cards only, or structured fields, files and intake as well?
  • Compliance evidence (15%): What the vendor publishes on its own trust pages, not what a directory claims.
  • Self-serve onboarding (15%): Can you get to a first token today?
  • Processor and platform independence (15%): Do tokens move, or do they lock to one provider?
  • Deployment and exit (10%): Regions, self-hosting, and how hard it is to leave.

This is an editorial comparison published by one of the vendors in it, not an analyst ranking. Weight it differently and the order changes: A team protecting PHI at enterprise scale would push compliance and coverage up, which moves Skyflow itself back to the top.

On review scores: G2 and Capterra ratings here were checked on each product's page on 16 October 2026, and every one is reported with its review count. An average drawn from six reviews and an average drawn from forty-seven are not the same claim.

2. VGS

Best for: Teams that want to reduce card data exposure without rewriting their application.

VGS runs a processor-agnostic vault behind a reverse proxy that aliases sensitive data before it reaches your systems, then re-inserts the real value in real time on the way out.

The proxy is the draw. You can place VGS in front of an existing integration and leave most of your application untouched, which suits teams with a working payment flow they do not want to rebuild. It is also the trade-off: A proxy is a dependency in your request path.

Key features

  • Reverse-proxy aliasing that swaps sensitive values before they reach your servers, then re-inserts the real data on the outbound call, so your application logic does not change
  • A processor-agnostic vault holding cards, PII and files in one place rather than splitting them across separate tools
  • Network tokens issued and managed for you, which keep stored credentials current and can improve authorisation rates
  • Account Updater, which refreshes expired or reissued cards automatically so recurring charges stop failing silently
  • 3D Secure, BIN lookup and account validation, covering checks most payment teams otherwise buy separately
  • Wallet payments and agentic commerce tooling for newer checkout paths
  • An AI Data Firewall for teams routing sensitive records into model calls
  • VGS Collect for client-side capture and Control APIs for server-side policy
  • A free tier to test the integration before the paid plan starts

Pricing

VGS publishes plans starting at $1,000 a month, with a free tier to begin.

Pros

  • Less application rework than a direct vault integration
  • Deepest payments feature set in this comparison
  • A starting price is published, which most of this market does not do

Cons

  • The highest published entry price here
  • The proxy adds a network hop and an operational dependency in the payment path
  • Weighted toward payments, so a pure PII or PHI use case is a less natural fit than Skyflow

What users say

  • Likes: Fast integration and helpful support: A developer describes understanding the proxy quickly and moving their workflow onto it, with chat support singled out (source), and another reviewer credits the team for supplying the documentation needed to prove PCI compliance (source).

3. Evervault

Best for: Teams that need to model cost per operation before they sign.

Evervault is encryption-based rather than vault-based, built on a dual-custody model: You hold encrypted cards without the keys, Evervault holds keys without your cards.

It publishes a rate for every operation, which matters more than it sounds. If your workload reads or writes in an unusual pattern, Evervault is the only vendor here where you can work out the bill in advance rather than discovering it.

Key features

  • Dual custody, splitting the encrypted card and the key between you and Evervault so neither side holds both
  • Secure iframes for collecting and revealing cards without raw data reaching your frontend
  • Relay, an encrypted proxy that decrypts in transit at the last moment before a third-party API
  • Enclaves on AWS Nitro for running your own code against encrypted data in isolation
  • Fingerprint, returning card metadata and BIN details without exposing the number
  • A Network Tokens API, 3D Secure and Account Updater across the payments lifecycle
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay for wallet checkout

Pricing

Platform is $995 a month plus usage, recommended for under 25,000 transactions a month. Encrypts, relays and functions are unlimited. Decrypts are $0.005 each, file decrypts $0.02, fingerprints $0.005, and successful 3DS authentications $0.03 with nothing charged for failures. A Custom tier covers 25,000+ transactions with volume discounts, 24/7 support and SAML SSO.

Pros

  • Per-operation rates published in full detail
  • Dual custody is a genuinely different security posture
  • Enclave-backed processing for sensitive compute

Cons

  • The $995 base means decrypt charges sit on top of a four-figure floor
  • Encryption-based rather than vault-based, so migration from Skyflow is not a straight token swap
  • Cards are the centre of gravity, not general PII

What users say

  • Likes: Friendly UI and easy relay setup: A reviewer calls onboarding a breeze, with a modern UI and smooth inbound and outbound relay setup (source), and another values not having to manage encryption keys (source).
  • Dislikes: Pricing and narrow scope: One reviewer says pricing was not up to the mark (source), and another finds the product's purpose narrow next to more established tools (source).

Rated 4.4/5 on G2 from 17 reviews, checked 16 October 2026. No Capterra listing found.

Watch for: Read-heavy workloads. Unlimited encrypts with metered decrypts favours write-once, read-rarely patterns.

7. Protegrity

Best for: Large enterprises protecting data across analytics and data platforms at high volume.

Protegrity is a vaultless data protection platform for large enterprise estates, offering tokenization, masking and encryption across data pipelines and AI workloads.

Vaultless is the real distinction. Tokens are derived cryptographically rather than looked up in a store, which removes the vault as a throughput bottleneck. Across hundreds of millions of rows that matters more than it does in a checkout flow.

Key features

  • Vaultless tokenization, where tokens are derived cryptographically rather than stored, so there is no token database to scale, shard or back up
  • Masking and encryption available alongside tokenization, letting you choose the protection method field by field
  • Protection applied at the source, so records arrive already tokenized in analytics and AI pipelines
  • Coverage across major data platforms and warehouses rather than a single application
  • Enterprise policy and governance tooling for controlling who can detokenize what at scale

Pricing

Protegrity does not list prices on its site, so the number comes from a conversation. There are three routes on the site itself: Contact Sales, Book a demo, or Talk to an expert.

It also runs an assistant on the homepage that explicitly invites pricing questions, which is the quickest way to get an indication without putting a call in the diary. There is an interactive demo too, if you want to see the product first.

Pros

How To Choose A Skyflow Alternative

What are you actually protecting? Cards only points to Basis Theory, VGS, Evervault or Spreedly. Structured PII points to Enigma Vault or Protegrity. Documents and intake point to Enigma Vault, which is the only tool here with a turnkey intake app.

Can you start without procurement? Enigma Vault has permanent free tiers. VGS and Evervault offer free starting points above a paid floor. Protegrity, TokenEx / IXOPAY and Skyflow all begin with a sales call.

What scale are you at? Hundreds of millions of rows in analytics favours Protegrity's vaultless model. Thousands to low millions of requests favours everything else here.

Where does your data have to live? Enigma Vault runs US and EU. Basis Theory's entry plan is US-only. Confirm residency before you shortlist, not after.

Do you need staff to see the real value? If authorized people must view a full PAN or document, you need controlled retrieval and an agent workflow, not just an API. That narrows the list considerably.

What Migrating Off Skyflow Actually Involves

Moving a vault is not a configuration change, and any vendor who says otherwise is selling.

Confirm the export path first: Get in writing how the incumbent exports protected values, in what format and over what channel, before you sign anything new.

Re-vaulting means re-tokenizing: Old tokens do not work in a new vault. Every system holding one needs updating, which is usually more work than the integration itself.

Cards add a step: Card data moves between two PCI-compliant environments and both providers must agree the process. Bring your acquirer in early, not at cut-over.

Dual-run before cutting over: Write to both vaults and reconcile. Budget for two vendors for a month or two.

Check what you lose: Exact-match lookup, format preservation and searchability differ between vaults. A field you can join on today may not be joinable after the move.
