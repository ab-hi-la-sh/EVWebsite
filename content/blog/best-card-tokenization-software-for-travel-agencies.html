What Is Card Tokenization Software for Travel Agencies?

Card tokenization software for travel agencies captures a client’s card once, locks the real number in a secure vault, and gives the agency a token to keep on the booking, so advisors can still pay suppliers without card numbers sitting in email, notes, call recordings, or the CRM.

  • Virtual card handling: Cards issued by OTAs, wholesalers, and corporate travel programs should be tokenized on arrival, just like a client’s card.

How a Client Card Should Move Through a Travel Agency

Most agencies can’t replace every airline, hotel, cruise, or consolidator portal, so the goal is to keep the advisor’s workflow and take the card number out of email, notes, call recordings, spreadsheets, and the CRM. A typical reference flow looks like this:

  1. The agency creates a client record and sends a branded secure intake link.
  2. The client enters the card on a vendor-hosted form, so it goes straight to the vault, not through your website or booking app.
  3. The vault returns a token and safe display details (such as the last four digits), which you store against the client and booking.
  4. The client’s authorization, permitted purposes, and expiry are recorded separately, because a token isn’t consent.
  5. For an integrated gateway or supplier API, the vault’s proxy swaps the real card into the request on the way out.
  6. For a supplier that needs a typed card, an authorized advisor opens it in a logged screen, never in email or notes.
  7. You set retention rules: delete on card expiry, client request, or account inactivity.

Passports, visas, and loyalty or trusted-traveler numbers follow the same pattern, which is why many agencies want one place for cards and travel documents.

Two cautions apply to every vendor above: vaulting doesn’t authenticate the cardholder on a phone or email booking, and some suppliers restrict third-party card use or require specific authorization evidence.

Common Mistakes Travel Agencies Make When Buying Card Tokenization Software

  • Tokenizing the web form but not the phone line: If advisors still write numbers down during calls, the riskiest copy is untouched.
  • Forgetting the manual supplier: A proxy is useless for a portal that needs typed entry, so plan a controlled view.
  • Leaving cards in PNR remarks and email: New captures go to the vault, but old copies stay until you clean them up.
  • Assuming tokenization means PCI-free: It can significantly reduce scope; your acquirer or QSA confirms the rest.
  • Forgetting call recordings: A card read aloud can sit in a recording or transcript long after the booking, so audit recordings.
  • Setting no retention rule: Decide when stored cards and passports are deleted, such as at card expiry or client request.
  • Treating the token as consent: Keep the client’s authorization per supplier and amount, since some suppliers demand proof of third-party use.

Questions to Ask a Card Tokenization Software Vendor Before You Sign

  • Which roles and plans let an advisor see the full card, and is each view logged?
  • Can the full card be shown without passing through our own booking app or server?
  • How do you handle the CVV, how long do you keep it, and when is it deleted?
  • Can the same platform hold passports and visas, with expiry, permissions, and an access log?
  • Can you capture cards from a phone call without the advisor hearing or typing the number?
  • Which gateways and supplier APIs can your proxy reach today, and which need custom work?
  • How do we import existing cards from our current vault, and how do we get them out if we leave?
  • Can you share your current PCI DSS AOC and SOC 2 report?
