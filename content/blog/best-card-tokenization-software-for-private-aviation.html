What Is Card Tokenization Software for Private Aviation?

Card tokenization software for private aviation is software that replaces a client’s card number with a token at capture and keeps the real card in a certified vault, so trip records, CRM, and inboxes hold only the token. The vault charges the card through your gateway when needed.

  • Who buys it: operations, charter sales, client services, finance, and security at flight departments, brokers, and jet-card programs, often collecting cards through an executive assistant.
  • Remote capture and card on file: a secure link, hosted form, or phone IVR, then one reusable token per client for quotes, deposits, repositioning legs, and incidentals.
  • Proxy charging and portability: the vault inserts the real card into your gateway request, tokens aren’t locked to one gateway, and existing cards in spreadsheets or legacy systems can be imported.
  • Documents and audit: expiring-link sharing for passports and manifests, plus a log of who captured, viewed, or charged each card.

How to Choose Card Tokenization Software for Private Aviation

Five questions sort this list quickly:

  1. How do cards reach you today? If by phone and assistants’ emails, weight capture (IVR or secure link) highest.
  2. How many cards do you already hold? Years of stored cards make migration the real project, so ask about bulk import.
  3. How many gateways and currencies do you charge through? One processor makes most vaults equal; several make token portability decisive.
  4. Who charges and approves the final invoice? If finance charges weeks after capture, you need a trip-record token and a proxy.
  5. Who sees passenger documents before departure? If brokers and ground vendors do, prefer expiring, revocable links over attachments.

On weighting: for most charter operators and brokers, capture and migration matter more than the length of a gateway list, because the risk lives in the card that’s read aloud, typed into a notes field, or kept in an old spreadsheet.

A good pilot is one real trip: build the client profile, collect passenger documents and a card, approve the charter payment, share one document by expiring link, and revoke it. A design that keeps cards off your systems can significantly reduce PCI scope, but your acquirer or QSA confirms the final scope and SAQ.

A vault doesn’t replace fraud, approval, and treasury controls on high-value charges, data minimization still has to coexist with manifest and recordkeeping rules, and no vault satisfies aviation, customs, or sanctions obligations automatically.
