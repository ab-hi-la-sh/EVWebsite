What Is Data Tokenization Software for Travel Agencies?

Data tokenization software for travel agencies swaps a traveler’s passport number, date of birth, trusted-traveler number, and other personal details for tokens, so the real values sit in a secure vault while your CRM, inbox, and booking notes hold only stand-ins.

A token is a random reference with no meaning outside the vault. Say a client’s passport number is stored as “tok_8f2c.” Your advisor sees the token and the expiry month; when the airline form needs the real number, an authorized person or system swaps it back, and the vault logs who did it.

Strip a typical client file down and you find six layers of personal data. Each one arrives in a different way and leaks in a different place.

Much of this exists because airlines need it. Under the TSA’s Secure Flight rule, 49 CFR 1560.101, covered aircraft operators must request a passenger’s full name, sex, date of birth, and Redress Number, and the Known Traveler Number once TSA tells them to.

The same section says carriers must make sure third parties that accept reservations on their behalf comply. For international arrivals, carriers send CBP an APIS manifest under 19 CFR 122.49a, which includes passport number, issuing country, expiry, citizenship, and the PNR locator.

The legal duties sit with the carriers, but agencies collect and forward these details every day, so they end up holding a copy. The question for software is where that copy lives and who can read it.
