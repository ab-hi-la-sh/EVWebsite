An LLM privacy proxy sits between your application and the model provider, replaces PII in each prompt with tokens before the request leaves, and restores the real values when the response comes back.

A support agent at a lending company pastes a customer's name, email, account number and recent transactions into the team's AI copilot. She just wants a faster reply. The draft comes back in seconds, and so does the problem: those details now sit with the model provider, in the copilot's trace logs and sometimes in an error report.

That paste is the leak. If you're building AI into your product, you've probably felt the pressure it creates:

  • Launch deadlines: The business wants AI features in customers' hands this quarter.
  • Privacy review: Security and legal won't approve customer PII reaching a third party they don't control.
  • Engineering drag: Each team writes its own scrubbing code, and none of it behaves the same way.

In this post, we'll explore how an LLM privacy proxy resolves all three at one point in your architecture. We'll cover where PII leaks today, how a proxy handles real production traffic, how it compares with gateways and DLP tools, and what to ask before you choose one.

What Is an LLM Privacy Proxy?

An LLM privacy proxy is a reverse proxy placed in front of LLM APIs that detects PII like names and emails in outgoing prompts, swaps it for tokens and restores the real values in the model's response.

Before going further, let's clarify what "proxy" means here.

Your application already sends requests to a model endpoint, such as OpenAI's or Anthropic's API. With a privacy proxy, it sends those same requests to the proxy's endpoint instead. The proxy reads the text, replaces the PII it recognizes, forwards the protected request to the provider and reverses the swap when the answer comes back.

For example, let's say your application asks a model to draft a payment reminder for a customer named Angela Brooks. Here's what each side sees:

WhoWhat they see
Application"Draft a payment reminder for Angela Brooks."
Model provider"Draft a payment reminder for [NAME: Ry0Ixd1]."
AgentA finished reminder addressed to Angela Brooks

The model did all the work. It never learned who Angela is.

Three properties separate a privacy proxy from a simple filter:

  • Inline position: Every request on the protected route passes through it, so protection doesn't depend on each developer remembering to call a library.
  • Reversibility: Tokens map back to real values inside your boundary, so outputs stay usable for the people reading them.
  • Provider neutrality: One proxy can sit in front of several model providers, so your privacy rules live in one place.

You'll also see the term used for two different designs. A reverse proxy runs at the API endpoint and protects traffic from your applications, wherever they run. A local forward proxy runs on one laptop or server and intercepts that machine's outbound traffic.

Production AI features almost always use the reverse proxy model. That's the design we focus on in this post.

Where PII Leaks in AI Applications Without a Privacy Proxy

PII leaks in AI applications at every component a prompt touches on its way to the model, including retrieval, provider APIs, logs, traces, tool calls and the outputs written back into your systems.

A generative AI feature is a chain of systems: the user interface, a prompt builder, a retrieval step, the model API, provider logs, tracing tools, tool connectors and wherever the output lands.

PII that enters at the start can be copied into several of those places before anyone notices.

Let's follow Angela through a contact-center platform that summarizes calls with an LLM:

  1. Angela's name, phone number and account reference start in the agent's notes.
  2. Next, the prompt builder adds her full CRM profile for context.
  3. Everything then reaches the model provider.
  4. For debugging, the platform's tracing tool stores the complete prompt.
  5. Finally, the summary lands in the ticketing system with her details repeated.

Five steps, and Angela's PII now lives in at least four systems. Here's how each leak point shows up:

Leak pointWhat happensIn Angela's case
OversharingMore data goes into the prompt than the task needsFull profile pasted when only her payment date mattered
Provider exposureRaw identifiers reach the model APIName, phone and account number sent to the provider
Logs and tracesPrompt and response bodies are stored for debuggingCall notes kept in full inside the tracing tool
Retrieval overreachRAG pulls whole records into contextCRM lookup adds 40 fields to answer one question
Tool-call leakageAgents pass PII to other servicesAddress sent to a third-party lookup tool by an agent
Output reproductionPII reappears in what the model writesAccount number copied into the ticket summary

This isn't a niche concern. The OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications lists sensitive information disclosure as LLM02, and NIST's Generative AI Profile names leakage and unauthorized disclosure of personal data among the privacy risks of generative AI.

Why Do Teams Route LLM Traffic Through a Privacy Proxy?

Teams route LLM traffic through a privacy proxy to unblock compliance reviews, keep identifiers out of provider logs, honor erasure requests and stop rebuilding privacy controls inside every AI feature.

Now that we've seen where PII leaks, let's look at how those leaks turn into business problems for the teams building AI features:

  • Stalled reviews: Security and legal hold AI launches because they can't sign off on customer data reaching a third party.
  • Provider logs: Retention and jurisdiction at the provider decide what happens to anything you send. For law firms, privilege doesn't survive a provider log.
  • Contract limits: Data processing agreements and BAAs assign responsibility once data has moved. Neither stops the data from moving.
  • Erasure requests: Under GDPR or CCPA, customers can ask you to delete their data, and copies inside provider logs are hard to reach.
  • Duplicated controls: Without a shared layer, every AI feature ships its own detection code, and each version drifts.
  • Broken outputs: Redaction leaves blanks or generic labels, so the model can't tell people apart.

The erasure problem shows why a proxy's design matters.

For example, let's say Angela asks you to delete her data six months after her account closes. Here's what happens with and without a privacy proxy:

Angela's deletion requestWithout a privacy proxyWith a privacy proxy
What the provider holdsHer name, phone and account number in old promptsTokens that mean nothing on their own
What you can deleteYour own records onlyRecords you hold plus the token mappings in the vault
What's left at the providerReadable PII you can't recallOrphaned tokens that point to nothing

Because the provider only ever held tokens, deleting the mapping is enough. That's a much easier answer to give a regulator.

How an LLM Privacy Proxy Handles a Request

An LLM privacy proxy handles a request by intercepting it, detecting PII in the prompt, replacing each value with a token, forwarding the protected prompt and restoring real values in the response.

Let's stay with the contact-center platform. A collections agency uses it, and after each call, an LLM writes a summary for the next agent who speaks with Angela.

Here's what happens when that request runs through a privacy proxy:

  1. Intercept: Your platform sends its normal API request, now addressed to the proxy endpoint.
  2. Detect: Messages, the system prompt and any tool-call content are scanned for the entity types you've enabled.
  3. Tokenize: Each recognized value becomes a token, and the original is stored in an encrypted vault.
  4. Forward: Only the protected prompt reaches the model provider, which writes a summary using tokens.
  5. Restore: On the way back, the proxy swaps tokens for real values before the summary reaches the agent.

Here's the same call from each side. Token formats are illustrative.

Who sees itContent
Agent's call notes"Spoke with Angela Brooks, 555-0193, about account CA-88214. She'll pay on the 15th."
Model provider"Spoke with [NAME: Ry0Ixd1], [PHONE: Lm4Qz8], about account [ACCOUNT: 7Qp2mX]. She'll pay on the 15th."
Next agent"Angela Brooks agreed to pay on the 15th. Confirm the payment plan on account CA-88214."

Notice what stayed in the prompt. The payment date and the agreement are exactly what the model needs to write a useful summary, so they pass through untouched.

Identity is the only thing removed.

One exception is worth calling out. Card numbers shouldn't enter prompts at all, tokenized or not. Keep card capture in a separate, PCI-scoped payment flow.

For your engineering team, adopting a hosted reverse proxy usually means changing one setting in the SDK you already use:

BEFORE
# Before: PII goes straight to OpenAI
client = OpenAI(api_key="sk-...")
AFTER
# After: PII is intercepted and tokenized by NoPII
client = OpenAI(
  api_key="sk-...",
  base_url="https://api.nopii.co"
)

The rest of your application stays as it is.

How an LLM Privacy Proxy Handles Streaming and Multi-Turn AI Workflows

An LLM privacy proxy handles streaming and multi-turn workflows by restoring tokens as response chunks arrive, keeping each person's token consistent across turns and blocking requests it cannot protect.

A single request is the easy case. Production AI features stream their answers, hold long conversations and call tools halfway through a task.

Each of those behaviors puts different demands on a proxy. This is where weaker tools tend to break.

Streaming Responses

Chat interfaces stream text in small chunks through server-sent events (SSE). If a proxy waits for the full response before restoring values, the chat freezes and then dumps the whole answer at once.

A streaming-aware proxy restores tokens as each chunk arrives:

STREAMING RESTORE
Provider sends:   "Hi [NAME: Ry0Ixd1], thanks for confirming"
Agent sees:       "Hi Angela, thanks for confirming"

Provider sends:   " your plan on [ACCOUNT: 7Qp2mX]."
Agent sees:       " your plan on CA-88214."

There's a subtle catch here. A token can be split across two chunks, with "[NAME: Ry0" in one and "Ixd1]" in the next. NoPII handles this by holding back the last few characters of each chunk until it knows whether a token is complete, then flushing everything when the stream ends.

Multi-Turn Conversations

Conversations fall apart when the same person gets a different placeholder in every message. Deterministic tokens fix that by giving the same value the same token each time.

TurnPer-request placeholdersDeterministic tokens
1: "Angela called about her plan"[PERSON_1][NAME: Ry0Ixd1]
4: "Angela's husband Mark called too"[PERSON_1], [PERSON_2][NAME: Ry0Ixd1], [NAME: Pq7Lk2]
9: "Remind Angela about Friday"[PERSON_1], which may now point to anyone[NAME: Ry0Ixd1]

With per-request placeholders, the model can mix up Angela and her husband by turn nine. With deterministic tokens, it tracks both of them correctly for the whole conversation.

Agents and Tool Calls

AI agents gather data and act on it, which gives PII more places to travel. A typical renewal agent might:

  1. Pull Angela's record from the CRM.
  2. Call a scheduling tool to book a follow-up.
  3. Draft an email confirming the new date.

Each step sends content to the model or passes arguments to a tool. When detection covers tool-call content, Angela's details stay tokenized at every step, so her phone number doesn't end up inside an argument the model generated.

Failure in Both Directions

Every protection layer eventually hits an error. What matters is which way it fails:

  • Outbound: If detection or tokenization fails, the request should be blocked. A proxy that forwards raw data when something breaks gives you a false sense of safety.
  • Inbound: When restoring fails, the response should come back with tokens still in place. Nothing sensitive leaks, and the reader sees an obvious placeholder.

When NoPII can't tokenize a request, it stops the call and returns a 503 error that reads, "Request blocked to prevent data leakage." Your application can retry safely, and nothing reaches the model.

Labels That Trigger Refusals

Models sometimes refuse or hedge when a prompt names a sensitive category. Context phrase neutralization rewrites those labels, so the model has no reason to refuse and can't guess what a token hides:

BEFORE
Update the record for Angela Brooks (social security number: 123-45-6789)
AFTER
Update the record for [NAME: Ry0Ixd1] (ID number: [GOV_ID: k8Lm2n])

Which AI Traffic Should Run Through an LLM Privacy Proxy?

Any AI traffic your application sends to a model API should run through an LLM privacy proxy, while consumer chatbots, browser extensions and image or audio files need separate controls.

A privacy proxy protects the route you integrate with it.

That's its strength, because protection becomes part of how your application calls a model. It's also its boundary, because traffic that never touches the proxy never gets protected.

AI trafficRoute through the proxy?Why
Customer-facing AI featuresYesChatbots, reply drafting and summaries carry the most customer data
Internal copilotsYesStaff paste customer records into internal tools every day
RAG and retrieved documentsYesRetrieved text travels inside the API request, so the proxy can inspect it
Agent tool callsYesAgents build prompts and tool arguments from live records
Fallback providersYesUnprotected backup routes undo the protection on the primary one
Batch jobsYes, after testingConfirm the batch endpoint is supported before routing production volume
Consumer chatbot websitesNoTyping into a browser tab never touches your application's route
Browser extensionsOnly if rebuilt to call the protected endpointExtensions usually send prompts straight to the model vendor
Images, audio and attachmentsNoText-based detection doesn't read them, so extract the text you need first

Coverage depends on discipline as much as on the proxy.

For example, let's say your support app runs through the proxy, but a fallback route sends requests straight to a second provider during outages. Every outage now leaks PII, while your dashboard still shows the main route as protected.

We also advise minimizing data before it reaches the proxy. A privacy proxy should make it safer to send what a task needs. It should never become the reason to send whole records by default.

LLM Privacy Proxy vs AI Gateway vs DLP vs Redaction Library

An LLM privacy proxy tokenizes and restores PII in application traffic, while AI gateways manage routing, DLP tools watch employee activity and redaction libraries strip data inside one app.

These four categories overlap in marketing, which makes buying decisions harder than they need to be. Let's clarify what each one was built for.

  • AI gateway: Manages model traffic, including routing between providers, API keys, rate limits, caching and cost tracking. Many gateways offer PII masking as a plugin, but it's usually one-way, so original values don't come back.
  • DLP tool: Watches how employees move data through endpoints, browsers and email. That makes it the right control for consumer chatbots.
  • Redaction library: Code your developers call inside each application to strip PII. Every team maintains its own copy.
  • LLM privacy proxy: Sits in front of model APIs, tokenizes PII in prompts and restores real values in responses.

Here's how they compare side by side:

QuestionLLM privacy proxyAI gatewayDLP toolRedaction library
What job is it built for?Protecting PII in model callsRouting, cost and access controlStopping employees from leaking dataRemoving PII inside one app
How is data protected?Deterministic tokensMasking or blocking, often as a pluginBlocking, alerts and warningsDeletion or generic labels
Do real values come back in responses?YesUsually noNot applicableUsually no
What happens if detection fails?The request should be blockedDepends on configurationSet by security policyLeft to each developer
Does it cover consumer chatbots?NoNoYesNo
Who maintains it?The proxy providerYour platform teamYour security teamEach application team

Most mature AI programs use more than one. A gateway handles routing and spend, a privacy proxy protects the data inside each prompt, and endpoint DLP covers staff using consumer AI tools.

Hosted, Self-Hosted or Local: LLM Privacy Proxy Deployment Models

LLM privacy proxies run as hosted services, self-hosted software or local forward proxies, and the choice decides who secures the token vault, maintains detection and produces audit evidence.

Before comparing models, it helps to know where the real risk sits.

The most sensitive part of any privacy proxy is the token vault. It holds the mapping from every token back to the real value, so whoever runs the vault carries the security burden. That's the real difference between the three models.

FactorHosted managed proxySelf-hosted proxyLocal forward proxy
Who runs the vaultThe proxy provider, on certified infrastructureYour team, in your cloudEach machine, usually in local storage
Setup effortA base URL changeInfrastructure, key management and integration workDesktop app or proxy settings per machine
What it protectsAll application traffic on the protected routeAny application traffic you route through itOne device's outbound traffic
Audit and retentionBuilt inYou design and build themUsually minimal
Ongoing upkeepHandled by the providerDetection tuning, provider API changes, vault securityPer-device updates
Best fitProduct teams shipping AI features to productionTeams with strict residency mandates and platform engineers to spareIndividual developers and experiments

Self-hosting looks attractive until you list what it involves. Open-source proxies handle detection and token swapping, then leave the hard parts to you:

  • Encrypting the token map and managing its keys
  • Writing audit logs and retention rules
  • Tuning detection as new data formats appear
  • Updating integrations when providers change their APIs

Each piece works on its own. The seams between them are where data leaks.

A hosted proxy moves that burden to a provider whose whole job is running it. You keep your own model provider keys, your application changes one setting, and the vault inherits the provider's security certifications.

LLM Privacy Proxy Use Cases by Industry

LLM privacy proxy use cases span customer support, financial services, legal, insurance, HR and accounting, wherever applications send customer, client or employee records to a model API.

The pattern is the same across industries. What changes is the data, the workflow and the regulator watching it.

Customer Support and SaaS Platforms

A help-desk software company adds AI reply drafting and ticket summaries for its customers. Every ticket carries names, emails, phone numbers and account IDs, and chats run at high volume with streaming responses.

Routing those calls through a privacy proxy changes three things:

  • Speed: Streaming restore keeps replies appearing at normal pace.
  • Coherence: Each customer keeps one token across a conversation, so summaries stay accurate.
  • Erasure: Purging a customer's tokens leaves nothing meaningful in provider logs when a deletion request arrives.

Financial Services and Fintech

Support agents at a lending platform paste account details into an internal copilot many times a day. Engineers on the same team paste API keys and connection strings into AI coding assistants while debugging.

A privacy proxy with secrets detection catches both. Token formats are illustrative:

ENGINEER PASTES
Why does this fail? key=sk_live_51Hx... db=postgres://admin:pass@prod-db
MODEL RECEIVES
Why does this fail? key=[SECRET: a8Kd2p] db=[SECRET: Qw9Lm3]

Fraud teams get the same benefit when they summarize transaction narratives, because customer names and account numbers stay tokenized throughout.

Law Firms and Legal Tech

A personal injury practice uses AI to build medical chronologies from claimant files. Those files hold client names, opposing parties, matter numbers, medical history and sometimes minors' records.

Legal AI taskWhat the proxy tokenizesWhat the model still works with
Chronology buildingClient and provider names, dates of birthTreatment sequence and dates of care
Demand draftingParty names, addresses, matter referencesInjuries, liability facts and damages
Deposition summariesWitness names and contact detailsTestimony and key admissions

Deterministic tokens keep a long chronology coherent, with each person tracked as one token across hundreds of pages. Bar ethics guidance asks firms to assess confidentiality before adopting AI tools, and a proxy gives that assessment a concrete data flow to point to.

Insurance Carriers, TPAs and MGAs

A single claims file can hold medical records, bank details, a driver's license and payment information. Here's how an adjuster's AI assistant handles one with a proxy in place:

  1. First, the adjuster asks for a summary of a 60-page claim file.
  2. Next, the proxy tokenizes identifiers across every regulated category in one pass.
  3. Then the model summarizes injuries, coverage questions and next steps using tokens.
  4. Finally, the adjuster reads the summary with real names restored, while the audit log records every detection for market-conduct exams.

HR and People Platforms

HR tech vendors are building recruiting assistants that screen resumes and summarize interview feedback. Many of these pilots start before legal has reviewed the tool.

That's a problem, because candidate names, contact details and salary figures belong to people who never agreed to share them with an AI provider. A proxy tokenizes them before the model scores or summarizes anything, and the audit trail gives legal the evidence it needs to approve the pilot.

Accounting and Tax Firms

Every spring, accounting firms collect tax returns, bank statements and payroll records by the thousand. When a firm uses AI to review those documents and draft summaries for preparers, tax IDs and bank account numbers get tokenized in the extracted text.

Preparers get usable summaries. The provider never holds a client's financial identifiers.

How to Choose an LLM Privacy Proxy: 8 Questions to Ask Vendors

Choosing an LLM privacy proxy means asking vendors how it fails, how it handles streaming, how consistent its tokens are and what audit evidence, retention controls and agreements it offers.

Feature pages tend to look alike. The fastest way to separate vendors is to ask how their proxy behaves when things get difficult.

Bring these questions to your next vendor call:

QuestionWhy it mattersA strong answer
What happens when detection fails?Forwarding raw data on error leaks silentlyBlocked request plus a logged event
Are values restored in streamed responses?Chat features freeze without streaming restoreChunk-by-chunk replacement, with supported providers named
Does the same value always get the same token?Conversations and agents lose track of people without itDeterministic tokens across a conversation
Can we add our own entity rules?Account numbers and claim IDs follow local formatsConfigurable entity types plus custom phrase rules
Is secrets detection included?API keys leak through prompts as often as names doCredentials detected in the same pass as PII
Will our providers and frameworks work through one route?Fallback providers and agents need the same protectionYour providers, plus LangChain or LiteLLM, on one endpoint
How detailed is the audit log?Reviewers want evidence for every requestEntity type, confidence score, provider and model, exportable
Can we control retention and sign the agreements we need?Erasure requests and regulated data depend on itToken retention controls, purge on request and a BAA where needed

Then test before you buy. Take a set of synthetic or approved prompts from your real workflow and replay them through each shortlisted proxy.

Measure five things:

  1. Entity recognition across your real formats
  2. Token consistency across turns
  3. Restoration accuracy in responses
  4. Added latency on your workload
  5. Sensitive context left after tokenization

A week of replay testing usually settles an evaluation faster than a month of feature comparisons.
