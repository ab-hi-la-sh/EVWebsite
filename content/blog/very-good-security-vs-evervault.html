Very Good Security (VGS) and Evervault are both developer-first platforms that keep card numbers off your own servers to shrink your PCI DSS scope, either by holding the card in a vault or by encrypting it before it reaches you.

VGS is best for teams that want one vendor to hold the cards and supply a deep card toolkit, while Evervault is best for engineering teams that want to keep encrypted card data in their own database and run their own code on it.

Where the Vault Model Helps: VGS is the one place your cards live, so there is a single store to audit, and VGS can run network tokens and account updater directly against the cards it holds. The trade-off is a provider-to-provider card migration if you leave.

Where the Dual Custody Model Helps: Evervault’s FAQ says it cannot decrypt your data without your app’s API key, so a breach of either side alone exposes nothing readable. There are exceptions worth asking about, since features such as enrolling cards for account updates create card objects on Evervault’s side.

Where Each Costs You: With Evervault you never hand over the dataset, yet you still depend on Evervault to decrypt it. With VGS you hand over the dataset, and you depend on VGS to release it. Neither is free of lock-in.

If your security team wants a single vendor-held store with the deepest network tools, the vault model is simpler. If it refuses to hand the card dataset to a third party, the dual custody model fits. Our guide to vault vs vaultless tokenization covers the broader trade-off.

Where VGS and Evervault Differ in Practice

Card Capture on the Web and in Apps

VGS Collect provides hosted form fields for JavaScript, iOS, Android, and React Native, and it can also take data over HTTPS or SFTP. A VGS inbound route strips card data from requests and replaces it with aliases before your servers see them, so you can protect existing endpoints mostly through route configuration.

Evervault’s Card, PIN, and Reveal components cover JavaScript, React, React Native, iOS, and Android, and they encrypt the card on the device. Relay does the same interception as a VGS inbound route, except what it hands your servers is ciphertext you keep. Relay can also deliver data to your frontend, which helps when you need to show a stored card back to a customer.

For most checkouts either one works, and both offer more ready-made pieces than Enigma Vault’s hosted forms and API.

Network Tokens, Account Updater, and Agentic Tokens

Network tokens keep stored cards working after reissue and can lift approval rates, so they matter most to subscription and card-on-file businesses. VGS leads with them.

Its comparison page describes direct connections to all four card networks, and its agentic tokens page calls VGS the largest independent provider of agentic tokens, live across Visa, Mastercard, and American Express. It also links to agentic commerce hub pages for agents and merchants.

Evervault’s docs list Visa, Mastercard, and American Express network tokens, with Discover marked as coming soon, so if a large share of your customers pay with Discover, that gap is worth a direct question.

Its account updater is $0.25 per card update, with a real-time updater also listed as coming soon.

For AI agents, Evervault sells Browser Tokens, which let an agent complete checkout on a merchant’s website without seeing the card number, at $0.25 per card session plus $0.05 per credential and $1 per GB of bandwidth.

Network-level credentials suit merchants that accept them, and browser-level checkout reaches sites that do not.

Custom Code: VGS Compute and Evervault Functions and Enclaves

If you only forward card data to a processor, you may never need custom code. If you have to transform, validate, or sign sensitive values, this is where the two diverge most.

  • VGS Compute: Runs your logic inside VGS’s proxies on data in transit, for payload transformation, custom cryptography, or policy checks. It is listed on the Growth package, so it comes with a quote.
  • Evervault Functions: Run Node.js or Python on decrypted data, with unlimited requests on the Platform plan.
  • Evervault Enclaves: Run your own Docker container inside an AWS Nitro Enclave, with no persistent storage and no shell access, priced by quote. Its customer stories include Morse, which built a non-custodial wallet recovery flow on Evervault.
Enigma Vault

Enigma Vault has no equivalent. It stores the card, then injects it into an allowlisted gateway request so the number never lands in your logs.

Payments Fintechs and Marketplaces With an Engineering Team

Subscription and Ecommerce Brands Storing Cards on File

Network tokens and account updater protect recurring revenue, and both vendors sell them: bundled into Growth at VGS and metered per operation at Evervault. If Your Agency, Travel Business, Clinic, or Support Team Takes Cards by Phone

A proxy protects data arriving over HTTP. It does nothing for a card number read aloud on a call and typed into a ticket. Neither VGS nor Evervault packages a phone channel. Enigma Vault’s Card Vault includes Twilio Pay IVR from the Growth tier, so callers enter their card on the keypad and agents never hear it, from $249 a month.

The last row matters for all three, and most for Evervault buyers, who often assume that holding the ciphertext means an easy exit. You still need Evervault to decrypt the data for a provider-to-provider migration, so agree how that works before you sign.

Moving From VGS or Evervault to Enigma Vault

If the price or the feature set no longer fits, the move is a card migration between two PCI-compliant providers, which both vendors and your acquirer need to agree.

  • Map Your Aliases or Ciphertext First: List every table that stores a VGS alias or Evervault ciphertext, since neither works in a new vault.
  • Plan the Export or Decrypt: VGS exports vaulted cards, while Evervault ciphertext must be decrypted inside a PCI environment and sent directly to the new provider.
  • Check What You Rely On: If you use network tokens, account updater, 3D Secure, chargeback alerts, VGS Compute, or Enclaves, confirm your gateway covers them or that you can live without them.
  • Choose a Token Format, Then Run Both for a Few Weeks: Enigma Vault issues random tokens, with a Luhn-passable option if a legacy system checks card formats. Then cut over.

Our guide to migrating tokenization providers covers the Evervault-style path in more detail. If you want help planning it, book a migration call.

Final Verdict: VGS, Evervault, or Enigma Vault?

Both VGS and Evervault are serious platforms. Very Good Security is the stronger fit if network tokens across all four networks, agentic tokens, and vendor audit help drive your decision, and you are comfortable with one vendor holding the cards.

Evervault is the stronger fit if your security team wants the encrypted data in-house, your engineers need Functions or Enclaves, and you value a published price for every operation. I’d choose between them on custody first and price second, because their entry prices are within $5 a month of each other.

If neither floor makes sense for your volume, or you also need to protect phone payments, records, documents, or AI prompts, Enigma Vault covers those at published prices, with a free sandbox to build on. It does not have network tokens, account updater, 3D Secure, or custom code, so if your revenue depends on those, it is not the right choice.
