Nightfall vs Limina: Quick Verdict

Nightfall: An AI-native data loss prevention (DLP) platform. It watches prompts, copy and paste, and uploads into AI tools through browser plugins and a macOS and Windows endpoint agent, scans SaaS apps such as Slack and Google Drive through APIs, and offers a Developer API that can mask, substitute, or encrypt findings. Its Foundation and Premier editions are priced per user per year by quote.

Limina (formerly Private AI): A de-identification engine that detects 50+ entity types and can redact, pseudonymize, tokenize, or replace them with synthetic values across text, documents, images, audio, and DICOM. It runs as a single container in your own environment or as SaaS. Private AI rebranded as Limina in March 2026, and Limina says the API, features, and deployment did not change.

Where Nightfall and Limina Differ in Practice

Developer Experience and Time to First Call

Limina’s Starter plan is free for 75 calls a day at 10 a minute, which is enough to test accuracy on a sample of your documents, images, and audio clips, and its 4 MB file cap covers most PDFs and short recordings. It is not enough to benchmark throughput, and it runs on Limina’s hosted service, so it does not test the self-hosted container you would deploy.

Nightfall’s Developer API is free for 3 GB of scanned data a month, with up to 500 KB per request, which is generous for text and the right place to test its detectors and redaction modes.

It does not show you the browser plugins, endpoint agent, or SaaS connectors, which need a proof of value through sales. Nightfall’s API was formerly called Firewall for AI, so older tutorials use that name.

Deployment and Data Residency

Limina can run as a single container in your own environment, pulled from Docker Hub or delivered as an encrypted image export, with CPU and GPU builds and guides for ECS, EKS, and Kubernetes. Its pricing page says customer data is processed and discarded, not stored or logged.

That control comes with work: your team sizes the hardware, patches, monitors, and scales the container, and synthetic replacement needs a GPU container with 24 GB or more of memory.

Nightfall asks for none of this on the server side, but it does ask you to deploy browser plugins and an endpoint agent through tools such as Jamf or Intune, and every scan is processed in Nightfall’s cloud, which its security page names as US hosting. We found no on-premises or VPC option.

Data Types, Files, and Audio

Limina says it is built for messy data, including speech recognition errors, OCR mistakes, and handwritten forms. It transcribes WAV, MP3, and M4A audio before redacting, and it handles DICOM medical images, where identifiers can sit both in metadata and burned into the pixels. Its materials cite Providence Health reporting 99.5%+ accuracy on physician conversations, which is a customer claim in vendor marketing.

Nightfall scans file uploads across its channels and offers asynchronous file scanning in its Developer API, but we found no audio transcription or DICOM support in its docs. If your sensitive data lives in call recordings or imaging archives, this is the deciding difference.

Workforce Monitoring and Shadow AI

Nightfall watches what employees paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot, and what they share in Slack, Google Drive, GitHub, Jira, Confluence, Zendesk, and Salesforce. It can block, redact, or coach the user in the moment, and Premier adds hooks for Cursor, Claude Code, and VS Code, MCP discovery and inventory, and Claude Compliance API monitoring.

Limina has no equivalent: it only sees data your systems choose to send it, so it cannot catch a leak nobody routed through it.

Redaction, Tokens, and Restoring Values

Nightfall’s mask and substitute modes are one-way. Its encryption mode is reversible with your own RSA private key, and Nightfall does not store your keys, so the restore logic is yours. Limina offers reversible tokenization and pseudonymization, and its docs describe a re-identify route that puts original values back into an LLM’s response. Limina can also generate synthetic replacements that keep a dataset useful for analytics and model training.

Languages and Detection Accuracy

Limina names identifiers such as the Canadian SIN, Japanese My Number, UK NHS number, and EU tax IDs, and laws including HIPAA, GDPR, CPRA, APPI, Quebec Law 25, and PCI DSS. Its pricing page says 52 languages and its docs have used 49+. Nightfall does not publish a language count; its help center describes custom regex detectors for local formats.

Every accuracy figure is vendor-run or vendor-commissioned. Limina says general-purpose tools miss 13.8% to 46.5% of identifiers in a test of about 45,000 words, against 0.2% to 7% for Limina, and its April 2026 benchmark report puts its F1 at 0.938 against 0.777 for AWS Comprehend on a custom dataset, with recall of 0.939 against 0.698 on the same dataset.

It also cites 99.5%+ validated by Armilla AI and 99.96% validated by Aetion and Datavant for expert determination. Nightfall advertises 95% detection precision, which its Lakera alternatives guide says was measured on customer data during limited proofs of value, and we found no published recall figure.

Precision tells a security team how many alerts were real; recall tells a data team how many identifiers were caught. Run the same labeled sample through both and count misses by entity type.

Does Nightfall Compare Itself to Limina?

No. We found no comparison page naming the other on either nightfall.ai or getlimina.ai. The only third-party comparison of Limina we found is Protecto’s Protecto vs Limina AI page, which says its Limina claims were reviewed in November 2026. It makes several claims worth checking.

  • Limina uses a traditional NER-based approach: This is a framing dispute. Limina describes its own models as built for messy data and code-switching between languages, and only your own test data can settle it.
  • Limina offers selective unmasking through a re-identification API: This is consistent with the re-identify route in Limina’s docs.
  • Limina routes AI traffic through NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails or your own code and lacks session continuity: This fits what we found. Limina does not list its own gateway, and its docs focus on per-request processing.
  • Limina reviews and documents may still say Private AI: Protecto does not raise this, but buyers should: private-ai.com redirects to getlimina.ai, some tooling such as its status page still uses Private AI addresses, and contracts, DPAs, and audit reports signed before March 2026 may name the old entity.

Protecto competes with both, so treat its page as one more vendor view, and let only the claims you can test in a proof of concept count.
