Basis Theory and Evervault are both developer-first card platforms that keep usable card numbers out of your own systems and give your engineers an API to collect, store, and use them.

Basis Theory is best for engineering teams that want the vendor to hold the card and bill per stored token, while Evervault is best for teams that want to keep encrypted card data in their own database and pay per use.

This guide compares Basis Theory and Evervault on architecture, features, pricing, compliance, and reviews, with Enigma Vault alongside as a third option for teams that want published prices and a simpler start.

Basis Theory vs Evervault: Quick Verdict

Basis Theory: A payments-first token vault with Elements for web and mobile, a proxy that forwards vaulted data to any API, and Reactors that run your own code against vaulted data.

It publishes one plan at $995 a month for 20,000 stored tokens, then $0.05 per extra token, and its docs label its Network Tokens and Batch Account Updater as Enterprise, along with Universal 3D Secure, with no published price for any of them. It has no reviews yet on G2 or Capterra.

Evervault: An encryption platform where card data is encrypted before it reaches you, you store the ciphertext, and Evervault decrypts it only inside its own infrastructure: Relay for proxied API calls, Functions for short code, and Enclaves for full containers in AWS Nitro Enclaves.

Its Platform plan is $995 a month plus usage and bundles card collection, 3DS, network tokens, wallets, and Browser Tokens, each billed per use. It is rated 4.4/5 from 17 reviews on G2.

Go with Basis Theory if:

  • You never want card data, encrypted or not, in your own database.
  • You charge a modest number of stored cards many times each month, so a stored-token bill stays flat while charges climb.
  • ISO 27001 matters in your vendor review, since Basis Theory claims it and Evervault does not.

Go with Evervault if:

  • You store a large card base but charge each card only now and then, so per-decrypt pricing works in your favor.

Go with Enigma Vault if:

  • Your card volume does not justify about $12,000 a year in platform fees before usage.

How Basis Theory and Evervault Differ at the Core

Basis Theory is a token vault your application sends data to, with the payments tooling built around it, whereas Evervault encrypts data and hands the ciphertext back to you, keeping the keys on its side in a dual-custody model. That difference shapes what sits in your database, who you depend on to use it, and what you pay for.

Basis Theory’s Model: Nothing card-shaped lives in your systems, and the vendor handles key rotation and custody. The vault becomes the single place your cards live, which makes the exit process the most important clause in the contract.

Where Basis Theory and Evervault Differ in Practice

Developer Experience and Time to First Token

Both vendors let engineers start without a sales call. Evervault’s sandbox is free with no credit card, no sales call, and no time limit. Basis Theory has a free, self-serve test tenant that takes synthetic data only, with Elements and a proxy to build against.

Custom code is where they split. Basis Theory Reactors run your code against data that stays in its vault, for jobs such as reformatting a card for a partner or calling an API that the proxy cannot reach on its own.

Evervault splits the job in two: Functions run short Node.js or Python code on decrypted data, and Enclaves run a whole Docker container inside an AWS Nitro Enclave, with attestation checkable through Evervault’s open source clients.

If you want to move an entire service, such as a scoring model or an issuer integration, Enclaves go further than either Reactors or Functions, but they are priced on a custom basis, so budget for a quote.

Payments Depth and What Sits Behind Enterprise

For subscription and card-on-file businesses, network tokens and account updater keep stored cards working after a reissue. Evervault bundles them into its Platform plan at published per-use rates, so a team on $995 can turn them on and know the cost.

It also runs its own 3DS server, which it describes as EMVCo-certified, and charges only for successful authentications, which helps merchants facing strong customer authentication in Europe or high fraud rates in travel and digital goods.

Basis Theory’s docs label its Network Tokens and Batch Account Updater as Enterprise, its Universal 3D Secure as Enterprise, and its pricing page lists no price for any of them, so ask. Its pricing details page also says network tokens count toward your stored-token total. If these features drive your revenue, the price you can see today is Evervault’s.

Enigma Vault

Enigma Vault does not issue network tokens. Many gateways offer them, and Card Vault charges through the gateway you already use, so check your gateway before paying for them twice.

Data Types, Files, and PII

Basis Theory has card and bank-account token types and stores other values as generic tokens, but payloads are capped at 1 MB on the $995 plan, and PII and PHI options are Enterprise.

Evervault can encrypt any field or file on its Platform plan, with encrypts unlimited, file decrypts at $0.02, and field decrypts at $0.005. Because Evervault’s ciphertext lives in your database, your own retention and backup rules apply to it.

Governance, Logs, and Keys

Basis Theory keeps the keys and the cards. On the $995 plan, logs are kept for 24 hours, and custom log retention, SSO, and SAML are Enterprise.

Evervault keeps the keys and leaves the ciphertext with you, so neither side alone holds a usable card. SAML SSO, 24/7 support, and a solutions engineer come with Evervault’s Custom plan.

Marketplaces and subscription businesses that need to spot the same card on two accounts can do so on both: Basis Theory says it correlates tokens for the same card without touching the PAN, and Evervault charges $0.005 per fingerprint.

AI and Agents

Both vendors have moved toward AI agents that pay. Evervault’s Browser Tokens let an agent complete a merchant checkout without seeing the card, billed at $0.25 per card session plus bandwidth. Basis Theory lists agentic enrollments on Enterprise.

Both are early products, so ask which merchants, networks, and card types each supports today, and what happens when an agent session fails partway.

Deployment and Data Residency

Basis Theory’s $995 plan runs in one environment. On 16 November its pricing page described that plan as US-only, and on 19 and 22 November it read “one environment (US or EU),” so ask which region your tenant will run in.

Evervault offers US and EU Relay endpoints and runs Enclaves inside AWS Nitro Enclaves; ask about its EU processing commitments. Enigma Vault is managed in US and EU regions, with EU data residency an add-on from Card Vault Growth.

What Basis Theory’s Comparison Page Says About Evervault

Basis Theory publishes an Evervault comparison page and an Evervault alternative page with the same table. We checked each claim about Evervault against Evervault’s own pages on 22 November 2026.

  • Evervault is charged per decryption: Accurate. Evervault charges $0.005 per decrypt, and encrypts are unlimited.
  • Evervault has no native fingerprinting: Contradicted. Evervault’s pricing page lists fingerprints at $0.005 each.
  • The decryption key is kept on Evervault’s side: Accurate, and consistent with Evervault’s dual-custody model. The same page’s table also says you are “responsible for securing the decryption key yourself,” which contradicts its own text.
  • Evervault holds PCI Level 1, SOC 2 Type II, and HIPAA: Incomplete. Evervault also lists CSA STAR and EMVCo certification for its 3DS server, and says it signs BAAs rather than claiming HIPAA certification.
  • Network tokens and account updater must be decrypted and passed between systems: We found no Evervault documentation describing it that way; both are Evervault-run features billed per use.
  • Migration needs active participation from Evervault: Partly. Evervault documents a self-serve export enclave that re-encrypts data to your own key, though the keys still sit with Evervault until then.

The page also describes Basis Theory’s own 3DS, network tokens, and account updater as native to the vault without plan labels, while its docs place Network Tokens and Batch Account Updater on Enterprise, and it lists ISO 27701 and NACHA, which we did not see on its security page.

Evervault has no page naming Basis Theory. Treat both as marketing, and let only the claims you can test in a proof of concept count.

  • Agree the Transfer: From Basis Theory, cards leave the vault in a secure file transfer. From Evervault, its docs describe a decryption enclave that re-encrypts your data to your own key for export.
  • Rehome Custom Code: Reactors, Functions, and Enclaves need another home, since Enigma Vault does not run customer code.
  • Check Your Gateway: Confirm it covers network tokens, account updates, and 3DS if you rely on them.
  • Run Both, Then Cut Over: Write new cards to both for a few weeks, reconcile, then switch.

Our guide to migrating tokenization providers has a checklist for running two vaults side by side. If you want help mapping what you store, book a migration call.

Final Verdict: Basis Theory, Evervault, or Enigma Vault?

Basis Theory and Evervault are closer than most pairs: both are built for engineers, both start at $995 a month, and both can run your code on card data. Evervault is the stronger choice when you store many cards, want network tokens and 3DS at a visible price, or need full Enclaves.

Basis Theory is the stronger choice when you want no card data of any kind in your systems, need ISO 27001, or charge a small set of cards very often.

I’d put your stored-card count and monthly charge count into the cost table above, and I’d get a written quote with plan labels and overage terms from either one before you commit.

If your volume does not justify $995 a month, or you take cards by phone, Enigma Vault’s Card Vault starts free and its paid plans run from $49 to $499. It has no custom code, network tokens, or account updater, so if your project depends on those, it is not the right choice.
