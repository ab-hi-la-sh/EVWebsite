Credit card tokenization API

Accept payments without storing card numbers.

Card Vault is a credit card tokenization API with a payment gateway proxy. It tokenizes payment cards the moment they are captured and charges them through the payment gateway you already use. One way in, and no way out except through the proxy. Your PCI scope collapses to nearly nothing, and your gateway choice stays yours.

The problem

Holding cards puts your whole stack in scope.

Every server the data touches, every database it lands in, every backup it survives in. And your processor’s own vault solves the storage problem while creating a new one, because your card data is now locked inside one vendor.

Card Vault removes both problems at once. Cards never touch your systems, and charges route to any gateway you choose.

Tokens

Tokens that behave like cards. Without being cards.

Engineered so your existing systems keep working with no schema changes.

Luhn-passable tokens.

Tokens can pass card number validation, so checkout code, fraud rules, and legacy systems that expect a valid-looking card number never notice the difference.

Deterministic token option.

The same card can always map to the same token, preserving customer-level lookups, deduplication, and card-on-file logic.

Nothing inside.

Tokens are generated by a CSPRNG and encoded in Base64URL: 22 characters carrying 128 bits of entropy. No structure to reverse, no algorithm to attack.

Ephemeral CVV.

CVVs are held only long enough to authorize, then purged automatically after 30 minutes. A CVV is never stored, which is exactly what your assessor wants to hear.

The proxy

Process tokenized payments through your gateway.

The payment proxy sends charges to your gateway with real card data substituted inside the vault, so raw values never touch your servers.

Any REST gateway.

Authorize.net, Stripe, Braintree, PayPal, and any REST-based payment gateway. Switching processors later is a configuration change, not a migration.

Signed requests.

Every proxy request is HMAC-signed, with custom headers and HTTP methods supported, and domain whitelisting restricting where charges can be sent.

Template substitution.

Placeholders for card number, CVV, and expiry are replaced with real values inside the vault, then the CVV is purged automatically after authorization.
