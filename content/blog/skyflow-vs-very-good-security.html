Skyflow and Very Good Security (Very Good Security) are both developer-first platforms that keep sensitive data out of your own systems, Skyflow through a governed data privacy vault and VGS through a proxy that swaps card data for aliases.

This guide compares Skyflow and VGS on architecture, features, pricing, compliance, and reviews, with Enigma Vault alongside as a third option for teams that want published prices and a simpler start.

Skyflow is best for enterprises that need to govern PII, PHI, and card data across datastores, models, and agents, while Very Good Security is best for payment teams that want to protect card traffic through a proxy.

How Skyflow and VGS Differ at the Core

Skyflow keeps your data in a vault that you query and govern through an API, with a proxy option for outbound calls, whereas VGS puts a proxy in front of your traffic and swaps data for aliases on the way in and out.

Where Skyflow and VGS Differ in Practice

Governance and Access Control

Skyflow’s access model combines roles, row-level rules, and context-aware authorization, and it can return a value masked, redacted, or in plain text depending on who asks. Its documentation also describes running encrypted queries and aggregations without decrypting, and customer-managed keys (BYOK and BYOKMS) are supported on every deployment model.

Very Good Security controls access through IAM with SAML identity providers, tags and classifiers on stored data, and routes that limit which data classes reach which destinations. Its documentation does not describe customer-managed keys or searching protected data.

For a company where dozens of teams touch the same records, Skyflow’s model is the more granular. For a payments team that mainly needs to control which services see a card number, routes are enough.

Payments Depth

This is VGS’s home ground. Its payments line includes network tokens, account updater, card attributes with BIN lookup, 3D Secure through a single API, wallet payments, and a card management platform for issuing, and its pricing page lists several of those on the Growth package.

Skyflow’s documentation lists network tokenization, account updater, 3D Secure, and BIN lookup, and it connects to processors such as Stripe, Adyen, Braintree, Cybersource, and Checkout. A 2022 Skyflow post says network tokenization covers Visa, Mastercard, and American Express, while a same-day press release names only Visa, and neither states availability. Skyflow does not describe card issuing as a capability, and we found no Apple Pay or Google Pay support in its docs.

If payments optimization is your main need, ask each vendor to demonstrate the specific features you will use.

AI and Agent Data

Skyflow’s homepage now leads with agents and “Runtime AI Data Control.” What is documented today is Detect, which de-identifies and re-identifies text, files, audio, and images, covers 60 entity types and more than 40 languages, and adds guardrails such as toxicity checks. Java Detect methods are labeled beta in its SDK docs. Skyflow announced MCP data protection in August 2025 and launched Skyflow for Glean on 25 August 2026. Its pages on agent memory, agent identity, and agentic commerce are marketing-only for now, with no documentation behind them that we could find.

VGS markets an AI Data Firewall, a proxy that tokenizes sensitive values in prompts, and an Agentic Commerce Hub with agentic tokens that run in its US environment. The firewall page lists no pricing, documentation, entity counts, or languages.

Time to First Token and Support

Very Good Security has a free, self-serve sandbox, though it is not for real data. Skyflow’s homepage offers a demo, and its free Try Account is request-gated: a team member contacts you to set it up. The Try Account is for non-production use with test data only, and it carries no support or SLA. Production access comes through a sales process or an AWS Marketplace private offer.

On support, Skyflow’s Terms describe standard support by default, with premium levels set in the order form and a Slack channel only on premium. VGS publishes no support tiers, and G2 reviewers often praise its chat support.

Skyflow vs VGS Pricing

What Skyflow Charges

Skyflow publishes no plans or prices, and its pricing URL simply shows the homepage. Quotes come through a demo or an AWS Marketplace private offer. A free Try Account exists for evaluation with test data only.

Skyflow’s Terms of Service, updated 15 July 2026, show how a contract works. Fees are based on a count of “Unique Identifiers,” such as a person, entity, or account number, and are invoiced annually in advance. Fees rise 5% a year at renewal, and an order form can set usage limits, with retroactive true-up charges for excess use at Skyflow’s then-current rates.

Buyer data is too thin to quote: Vendr has a single Skyflow purchase on record.

What VGS Charges

VGS publishes two packages. Starter plans start at $1,000 a month, with no included features, units, or overage listed. Growth has no price, and its card lists network tokens, account updater, a PCI compliance subscription, a VGS-provided auditor, large file transfers, VGS Compute, and card attributes. Vendr shows a median of $56,700 a year for Very Good Security, with a range of $21,600 to $132,000, and buyers there report that overage charges can be negotiated.

Moving From Skyflow or VGS to Enigma Vault

If the price or the contract terms no longer fit, the move is a data migration between two vendors, so plan it before you cancel anything.

  • Map What You Store: List every field, file, and route, and which team or service reads each one.
  • Check Your Governance Needs: If you rely on field-level policies per role, confirm that Enigma Vault’s access by client and action covers them.
  • Plan for Tokens That Do Not Transfer: Re-tokenize into the new vault and update every system that stores the old token or alias.
  • Run Both for a Few Weeks: Then cut over.
