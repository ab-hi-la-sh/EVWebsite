VGS is best for online businesses and fintechs that want a vault with card lifecycle tools, while TokenEx is best for enterprise and omnichannel merchants that want tokens and routing from one vendor.

VGS vs TokenEx: Quick Verdict

VGS: A processor-agnostic vault that collects card data through hosted forms and native mobile SDKs, intercepts it before it reaches your servers, and restores the real values on the way to your processor. Around the vault it sells network tokens, account updater, card attributes, VGS Compute, and a PCI compliance subscription with a VGS-provided auditor. Starter plans start at $1,000 a month, and Growth is by quote.

TokenEx: IXOPAY’s tokenization platform. TokenEx’s homepage now calls it “the tokenization platform from IXOPAY,” so contracts, support, and roadmap run through IXOPAY. TokenEx Vault secures payment, PII, and health data, and IXOPAY sells it as Universal Tokens inside IXO One, which adds orchestration across 250+ acquirers and 300+ payment methods, AI payments intelligence, and in-store P2PE. Prices are by quote.

Enigma Vault: A PCI DSS Level 1 tokenization service with a free Lite sandbox and Card Vault plans of $49, $249, and $499 a month. It adds phone card capture through Twilio Pay and charges through the gateway you already use, without network tokens, account updater, or routing.

Where VGS and TokenEx Differ in Practice

Payment Routing and Orchestration

This is TokenEx’s home ground. On IXOPAY’s own site, tokenization appears as Universal Tokens, one of three areas of IXO One, next to orchestration across 250+ acquirers and 300+ payment methods and AI payments intelligence. One vendor means one integration, one contract, and one place to see approval rates across processors, and orchestration can retry a declined payment across providers.

VGS keeps you processor-agnostic, so you can send the same card to different processors, but it does not sell a routing or retry engine. Routing rules stay in your own code or your gateway. Adding a processor means configuring an outbound route and writing the call, while with TokenEx you can use the Transparent Gateway the same way or one of IXOPAY’s prebuilt adapters, where the integration already exists.

If you only want a vault, ask IXOPAY plainly whether Universal Tokens can be bought without orchestration and what the price difference is. Its homepage says you pay for the capabilities and usage you need, which suggests it can, but it publishes no package list.

Payments Depth

Both vendors provision network tokens. VGS bundles them with account updater and card attributes in its Growth package and markets direct connections to all four card networks. IXOPAY provisions and manages network tokens inside Universal Tokens and says its tokens can be used with any gateway or acquirer. It also sells account updater, standalone or inside orchestration, and a BIN lookup that returns card attributes.

The practical question is where you want network token lifecycle to live: with the vault that holds your cards, or with the platform that also routes them. Ask each vendor for coverage by card network and region, and to demonstrate the specific features you will use.

In-Store and Phone Payments

IXOPAY says its P2PE solution for in-person payments is PCI-validated, and that tokens can unify payment data across in-person and online channels. For a retailer with stores and ecommerce, a card tapped at a terminal and a card typed at checkout can map to the same customer token.

VGS’s pages focus on online, in-app, and file-based card data, so if you run terminals, you would pair VGS with a separate in-store provider and reconcile tokens yourself.

Phone is the gap neither covers. Agencies, clinics, and support teams that still take card numbers over the phone end up reading them into a web form, which brings agents and call recordings into PCI scope. Custom Logic and Audit Help

VGS Compute, listed on Growth, lets you run your own logic on sensitive data inside VGS’s environment. We found no equivalent on TokenEx’s or IXOPAY’s pages; IXOPAY’s answer to custom logic is orchestration rules and its payments intelligence. If you need to transform card data in ways your processor does not support, VGS has the clearer story.

On audits, IXOPAY says its tokens can “eliminate up to 90% of PCI DSS requirements” and that its P2PE solution is PCI-validated. VGS sells the audit help itself: a PCI compliance subscription for Level 1 and Levels 2 to 4 and a VGS-provided auditor, both listed on Growth. Neither vendor’s claim replaces your acquirer’s or QSA’s view of your final scope.

Does VGS Compare Itself to TokenEx?

Not by name. VGS publishes a comparison of VGS against “other independent vaults”, and its table puts VGS in one column and an unnamed “Other Vaults” column in the other. TokenEx is never mentioned, but as an independent vault it falls inside that group, so the claims are worth checking against TokenEx’s own pages.

The page compares eight factors. These are the ones that bear most on a TokenEx decision:

  • Direct network connections: VGS says it connects directly to Visa, Mastercard, American Express, and Discover, and that other vaults “often go through 3rd parties.” TokenEx’s Universal Tokens page lists network token provisioning but does not say whether its connections are direct, so ask IXOPAY which networks it connects to itself.
  • Reliability: VGS cites four-nines availability, multi-region disaster recovery, active-active deployment, and cell-based isolation. It says other vault customers “often come to VGS for redundancy after costly outages,” with no named example.
  • Complex migrations: VGS claims bulk card migrations, with VGS Compute (its Larky language) for custom logic, and says other vaults have less experience with high-volume moves.
  • Enterprise customers: VGS says other vaults “are focused on long-tail and SMB.” That does not fit TokenEx, whose homepage shows enterprise client logos and which now sits inside IXOPAY, an orchestration platform that says it has orchestrated $200B in transactions.
  • Integrated payment products: VGS points to one Card Management Platform API for network tokens, account updater, account validation, 3DS, and card attributes. IXOPAY’s answer is orchestration across 500+ adapters, which is a different kind of integration.
  • Support: VGS describes a dedicated account manager, solutions architect, and project manager, with 24/7 coverage, and says other vaults have “limited full-time support.”

The page also lists headline figures: 10B+ tokens stored, 10B+ monthly interactions, zero security breaches, and 250K+ requests per minute at Black Friday peaks. It repeats VGS’s PCI DSS Level 1, SOC 2 Type 2, and ISO 27001 certifications and its Visa and Mastercard service provider listings. All of these are VGS’s own claims, with no third-party source on the page.

IXOPAY’s Universal Tokens page contrasts its tokens with processor-issued tokens that only work with one processor; it does not mention VGS. Treat both vendors’ positioning as marketing, and count only the claims you can test in a proof of concept.

Moving From VGS or TokenEx to Enigma Vault

A move is practical when you mainly use the vault and proxy, and harder when you rely on orchestration, P2PE, or network tokens. Plan it before you cancel anything.

  • Agree the Card Migration: Cards move directly between PCI-compliant providers, with your acquirer involved.
  • Plan for What Stays: Routing, retries, P2PE, network tokens, and account updater need another home or your gateway.
  • Re-Tokenize Stored References: VGS aliases and TokenEx Universal Tokens will not work in a new vault, so map old references to new tokens.
  • Run Both for a Few Weeks: Write to both, reconcile, then cut over.
