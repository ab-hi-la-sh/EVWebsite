What Does Data Tokenization Software Do?

Data tokenization software replaces sensitive values, such as card numbers, SSNs and health details, with tokens that have no usable meaning, and returns the original only to authorized requests.

Your application stores the token where the real value used to be. The real value lives in a secured vault, or you can recover it only through cryptographic keys, so a stolen database of tokens is worth very little to an attacker. Systems that hold only tokens often fall outside the scope of audits such as PCI DSS.

This guide covers data security tokenization only. I left out blockchain asset tokenization, language-model tokenization and masking-only tools, because they solve different problems. For the basics, read my guide to data tokenization use cases and how to choose a solution. For the architecture choice behind most of the tools below, see vault vs vaultless tokenization.

How I Evaluated the Best Data Tokenization Tools

I scored every tool on six criteria: token model, token formats, compliance evidence, searchability of protected data, integration and exit, and pricing transparency.

  1. Token model: vault-based, vaultless, proxy or encryption-based. This drives performance, portability and your audit scope.
  2. Token formats: random tokens, or format-preserving tokens that fit your existing database fields.
  3. Compliance evidence: the certifications and attestations the vendor publishes, such as PCI DSS, SOC 2 and HIPAA support.
  4. Searchability: whether you can look up or join records without detokenizing them.
  5. Integration and exit: APIs, SDKs, deployment options and how hard it is for you to leave.
  6. Pricing transparency: whether a starting price is public.

Where a vendor does not publish a fact on its own site, the card says so instead of guessing. Review scores are the ones G2 and Gartner Peer Insights showed on October 8, 2026. How to Choose Data Tokenization Software by Scenario

Match the tool to the data you protect and the team that runs it: payments teams start with card-focused vaults, SaaS teams with PII vaults and large enterprises with platform-level products.

  • Reducing PCI DSS scope: look at Enigma Vault Card Vault, TokenEx / IXOPAY, VGS and Basis Theory. All of them focus on keeping card numbers off your servers.
  • PII or PHI in a SaaS application: consider Enigma Vault Data Vault or Skyflow, which isolate sensitive fields behind access policies.
  • Enterprise databases and legacy formats: shortlist Thales CipherTrust, Protegrity and OpenText Voltage, which handle format-preserving tokens at scale.
  • LLM and AI pipelines: pick a tool that tokenizes personal data before it reaches a model. Enigma Vault NoPII and Skyflow both target this. See my guide to PII tokenization for AI.
  • Startups, SMBs and small budgets: start with a free tier. Enigma Vault's Lite plans and NoPII's free monthly tokens cost nothing, while several alternatives start near $1,000 a month.

Common Mistakes When Buying Data Tokenization Software

  • Treating encryption and tokenization as the same thing. You can reverse encryption with a key, while a token has no mathematical link to the original.
  • Ignoring exit costs. Ask how you would export your tokens and values if you left, and whether the token formats are portable.
  • Trusting marketing labels. Terms like zero-knowledge or bank-grade need a precise definition and evidence from the vendor.
  • Skipping governance on detokenization. Anything allowed to detokenize stays in your audit scope, so limit who and what can do it.
  • Buying without a pilot. Test latency, throughput and search behavior with your own data before you commit.
